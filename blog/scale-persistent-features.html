Correlation Stability (formerly Scale-Persistent Features): How Much Does Each Correlation Depend on Your Cases?

By Moonlit Social Labs · April 3, 2026 · 7 min read

You found a correlation. A natural next question is how much it depends on the particular people in your sample. If you dropped a quarter of them at random, would it still have the same sign?

Correlation Stability answers that question for every pair of variables. It adapts the case-dropping bootstrap and CS-coefficient that Epskamp, Borsboom and Fried (2018) introduced for psychological networks. It is not a new method, and it is not topological data analysis.

What It Reports

For each pair of variables:

  • Pearson r.
  • A Holm-adjusted p-value, corrected across all the pairs tested.
  • A 95% percentile bootstrap confidence interval from 2,000 resamples.
  • A stability coefficient, described below.

The stability coefficient

The tool drops 5%, 10%, and so on up to 75% of cases. At each level it draws 500 random subsamples and recomputes r. The coefficient is the largest share of cases that can be dropped while at least 95% of subsamples keep the same sign as the full-sample r.

Labels follow the cut-offs suggested by Epskamp et al. (2018):

  • Stable: coefficient ≥ 0.5.
  • Acceptable: coefficient ≥ 0.25.
  • Unstable: below 0.25.
What this can't tell you

Every number here comes from one sample. Dropping cases from that sample shows how precisely the sign of r is estimated. It moves closely with the p-value and the confidence interval: a large, precise correlation is stable, a small, noisy one is not. It cannot show that a relationship is real or that it will replicate in new data. In simulations with pure noise and n = 60, about 1 pair in 10 still comes out “Stable.” Treat the label as a summary of precision, and use the Holm-adjusted p-values and the CI alongside it.

When Should You Use It?

  • You are reporting a correlation matrix and want to show which estimates are precise enough to keep their sign under resampling.
  • You want a quick check before building a network or factor model on the same correlations.
  • A reviewer asks whether a correlation depends on a few cases.

Try It in PsyStat Lab

Correlation Stability is available in the Robustness Checks module of PsyStat Lab, and inside the Data Prep module. Load a dataset with several numeric variables to get the table for every pair.

References

  • Epskamp, S., Borsboom, D., & Fried, E. I. (2018). Estimating psychological networks and their accuracy: A tutorial paper. Behavior Research Methods.
